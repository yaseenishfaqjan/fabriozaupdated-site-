If you are buying to resell — into retail, to corporate clients, through a promotional distributor network or into your own warehouse — you are buying a supply relationship rather than a single order. Three things decide whether it works: the landed cost, whether run two matches run one, and whether your customer ever sees our name. All three are addressed below.

1. Your brand on the label. Never ours.

We are a cut-and-sew factory, not a brand competing with you. Everything we make ships with your woven labels, your hang tags, your packaging and your care labels. There is no FABRIOZA mark on the garment, inside it, or on the carton unless you ask for one.

We also do not publish customer work. Since 13 October 2026 our standing rule is that no customer's garments appear on this website at all, including in a portfolio. A non-disclosure agreement is signed on request, before you send artwork if you prefer. For a distributor whose own customers must never find the factory behind the line, that is the point rather than a footnote.

2. Factory-direct pricing, and where the margin actually comes from

You are buying from the facility that cuts and sews the garment. There is no agent, no trading house and no sourcing-office commission in the figure, which is usually 15–25% of a landed cost before anyone has made anything.

3. Test a line before you commit warehouse space

Our minimum is 50 pieces per style. Most factories quoting a distributor want 1,000 per style, which means your first stock decision is also your biggest one.

Run 50, put it through your channel, see what actually sells, and only then commit to 5,000 — with the same factory, the same patterns and the same approved sample as the reference. For a warehouse operation, the ability to validate a line cheaply before it occupies a pallet position is worth more than a percentage point on unit cost.

4. Will run two match run one?

This is the question that decides whether a supply relationship survives, so here is the straight answer in both directions.

What we control: we keep your approved sample and your graded patterns, so a repeat is made against a physical reference rather than a written description. Construction, measurements and fit repeat to the same tolerances: ±1 cm (±0.4 in) on body points, ±0.5 cm (±0.2 in) on small points, and ±5% on fabric weight.

What nobody controls: dye lots. Small colour variation between production runs is a property of fabric dyeing. If your line needs shade consistency across replenishment — and for a retail programme it usually does — the answer is to dye the full season's quantity in one lot and call it off in drops. Tell us at quotation stage and we will price it that way.

6. Quality control, and your own inspector

Four documented stages on every order: fabric inspection before cutting, in-line checks during stitching, a finished-garment measurement audit against the tech pack, and AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection with photo documentation that comes to you before the balance is due.

You are welcome to appoint your own inspector — SGS, Intertek, QIMA or your own staff — at fabric stage, in-line or pre-shipment. You choose them, you pay them, you receive the report, and we give them access to the floor. On a first programme with a supplier you have not met, that is reasonable, and it has never been a problem here.

8. What to send for a programme quotation

  • The styles, with a reference garment or picture for each.
  • Annual or seasonal forecast quantity per style, and the colourway split.
  • Fabric: composition and weight in GSM, or the handfeel you are matching.
  • Decoration and branding: labels, hang tags, packaging and any barcode or compliance labelling your channel requires — tell us the specification and we will confirm what we can produce rather than guess.
  • Destination and whether you want FOB or DDP.
  • Your replenishment pattern: one run, or drops across a season.

Send it to info@fabrioza.com and you will have a costed response within one working day, Monday to Saturday.

9. Questions distributors ask

Do you sell to the end customer in our territory?

We are a manufacturer and we quote whoever asks, but we do not run a consumer brand and we do not publish customer work, so we are not competing for your customer's attention. If you need territorial or customer-specific exclusivity on a developed style, raise it at quotation stage and we will put the terms in writing.

Can you hold a season's fabric for us?

Yes, where the quantity justifies it. Dyeing a season in one lot and calling it off in drops is the standard answer to shade consistency, and it is priced per programme.

Can you match a garment we already sell?

Post us the garment. Working from a physical sample is the most accurate brief there is, better than any tech pack, and we will tell you honestly where we can match it and where we cannot.

Are prices fixed for a season?

A quotation is valid for 30 days. On a forecast programme we will hold pricing for an agreed period and state it in writing, because a distributor cannot sell from a price list that moves underneath them.

Duty paid, courier of your choice, and we carry the risk

Duty paid (DDP) on every product, to every country. We settle the import duty and the customs clearance before your goods arrive, so there is nothing to pay on delivery and no courier invoice afterwards. Prefer to clear it yourself, or already have a broker? Take it duty unpaid instead — both are offered on everything we make, at any quantity.

Your courier. DHL Express when you want it fastest, UPS or FedEx when you have an account or a preferred carrier. Tracked either way, with the number sent the day it leaves.

If it does not arrive, that is ours to fix. We chase the courier claim ourselves, and we do not make you wait for it: a shipment lost in transit is remade and reshipped at our cost, or refunded in full including the shipping.
