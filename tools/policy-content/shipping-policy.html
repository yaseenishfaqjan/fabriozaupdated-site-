Every order ships by express courier with a tracking number, from Sialkot to your door. This page sets out who carries it, how long it takes, who pays the duty, and what happens if a shipment goes missing.

1. Who carries your order

We ship worldwide by DHL Express, UPS and FedEx, and you can choose. DHL Express is normally the fastest on our routes and is what we default to when speed matters; UPS and FedEx are there when you have an account with them, a preferred carrier for customs, or a rate we cannot beat. Tell us which you want and we quote it. If you have no preference we pick on speed and price for your destination and tell you which is carrying the order before it leaves.

Every shipment has a tracking number. We do not use unregistered or untracked post for commercial orders, whatever the quantity.

Transit time depends on the destination and starts when the courier collects the shipment, not when production finishes. It is separate from the production lead times in section 9 of our Terms and Conditions — 2 to 3 weeks for a sample, 3 to 4 weeks for bulk after sample approval.

These are the couriers' own service estimates. Customs inspection, a national holiday in the destination country, or a weather event at a hub can add days, and no courier guarantees a transit time on an international express shipment to every destination.

3. Duties and taxes: both options, on every product we make

Delivered duty paid is available on everything in our catalogue, to every country we ship to, at any quantity. There is no product line, no minimum and no destination where it is withheld. If you want a landed price with nothing left to pay, say so on the enquiry and that is the quotation you get.

This is the part that most often surprises a first-time importer, so both options are set out plainly. Which one applies to your order is stated on your quotation.

Option A — duties paid by the receiver on arrival

The courier contacts you, or your customs broker, before delivery and collects the import duty and any local tax. You pay it directly to the courier. Our price covers the goods and the freight only.

Option B — delivered duty paid (DDP)

We handle the duty, the customs entry and the clearance, and the shipment arrives with nothing to pay. There is no invoice from the courier on your doorstep. Where DDP is agreed, the duty is included in the figure on your quotation.

Import VAT or sales tax in your own country is a separate charge from duty, and remains payable by you even under DDP. A VAT-registered business normally recovers it. We cannot pay or reclaim another country's VAT on your behalf.

4. What shipping costs

Shipping is charged separately and is never included in a product price. An estimated shipping cost is given in every quotation, based on the weight and volume of the order and your destination.

It is an estimate until the goods are finished and weighed. Express freight is priced on volumetric weight as well as actual weight, so a large, light order of hoodies can cost more to send than its weight suggests. If the final figure differs from the estimate we tell you before dispatch, with the courier's rate.

5. Dispatch and tracking

Your order ships once the balance has cleared and you have approved the photographs and video of the finished goods.

The tracking number is sent to you on the day of dispatch, by email and WhatsApp, with the courier's name and the number of cartons. You can follow the shipment on the courier's own website from the point it is scanned at the Sialkot depot.

6. Delivery details are your responsibility

Please check the delivery address, the contact name and the telephone number on your quotation before you approve it. The courier uses exactly what is on the waybill.

Where a shipment is delayed, returned or needs re-delivery because the address was wrong or incomplete, because nobody was available to receive it, or because the consignee did not respond to the courier's customs or payment request, the courier's re-delivery, storage or return charge is payable by you. These charges are set by the courier and we pass them on at cost.

Tell us as early as possible if an address changes. Once a waybill is generated and the shipment has been collected, a change is not always possible and the courier may charge for it.

7. Lost or damaged shipments

Report a damaged carton to the courier at the moment of delivery, note it on the delivery record, and photograph the outer carton before opening it. That record is what a claim depends on, and a claim is difficult without it.

If your order does not arrive, that is our problem to solve, not yours. We open and pursue the claim with the courier ourselves, we keep you informed while it runs, and you do not have to deal with the courier at all.

And you are not left waiting on the outcome of that claim. Where a shipment is lost in transit, we remake and reship the affected goods at our own cost, or refund them in full, including the original shipping — your choice. Whether the courier eventually pays us is a matter between us and them.

This is deliberately more than a courier's own cover. Express carriers limit their liability by WEIGHT, not by what the goods are worth, so on a garment order their maximum payout is usually a fraction of the value. Leaving you to discover that after a loss is how a first order becomes the last one, so we carry the difference instead of selling you insurance against it.

Two things we do need from you, because a claim depends on them: tell us within 7 days of the expected delivery date if a tracked shipment has not arrived, and report a damaged carton to the courier at the moment of delivery.

A manufacturing fault is a different matter from transit damage and is covered by our Refund and Return Policy, which gives you 7 days from delivery to report it.

8. Export documentation

We supply the commercial invoice, the packing list and the certificate of origin where it is required, and we complete the customs declaration. Garments are declared accurately, at the price you paid.

We will not under-declare the value of a shipment or describe goods as a gift or a sample when they are not. It is illegal, and it would leave you exposed to a penalty in your own country rather than us.
