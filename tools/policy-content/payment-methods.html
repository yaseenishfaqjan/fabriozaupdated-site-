There are four ways to pay FABRIOZA. Card is the simplest and the one that gives you the most protection; bank transfer is usually cheapest on a large order. Whichever you choose, quote your invoice number so the payment reaches your order rather than sitting unmatched.

1. Card payment link — Visa and Mastercard

Ask for a card invoice and we send you a secure payment link. You open it, enter your card details on the payment page, and pay. Nothing is installed and no account is needed.

Card payments are processed by Safepay, a payment gateway licensed by the State Bank of Pakistan. The card details are entered on Safepay's own secure page. FABRIOZA never sees or stores your full card number — we receive confirmation that the payment succeeded, the amount, and the last four digits for reconciliation.

This is the method we recommend for a first order, and it is worth saying why: a card payment can be disputed with your own bank, so you have a route to your money that does not depend on our cooperation. A bank transfer has no equivalent protection. That is true of every factory, ourselves included.

2. Bank transfer in US dollars

We hold a FABRIOZA business account in US dollars with UBL (United Bank Limited), Pakistan. Transfer from your own business account by SWIFT.

The full bank details are on your invoice — account name, account number, IBAN and SWIFT code. They are deliberately not published on this page, so that nobody can copy them from the website and present them as their own. Always take them from the invoice we sent you.

Your bank, and any intermediary bank, may deduct a transfer fee. Please send the invoice amount net of your own charges, or add the fee, so the full amount arrives. An international transfer typically clears in 1 to 3 business days.

3. Remitly

Remitly is convenient for samples and smaller balances, and is usually cheaper and faster than a bank wire at that size. Send to FABRIOZA in Pakistan and send us the confirmation or reference number when it is done.

4. Western Union

Western Union works from almost anywhere and clears quickly. One step in their form confuses most people, so here it is directly:

  • Choose Cash Pickup.
  • When it asks for a pickup location, leave it blank or select no specific location. Pakistan is served by many agents and a specific branch is not required.
  • Complete the transfer, then send us the MTCN (the 10-digit tracking number), the exact sender name as written on the transfer, and the amount sent.

We cannot collect a Western Union transfer without all three. The sender name must match exactly, including spelling, or the agent will refuse the payout.

5. Always quote your invoice number

Put your invoice number in the payment reference, every time and on every method. It is the only thing that connects a payment to your order.

Several similar payments arrive each week. An unreferenced transfer has to be traced by amount and sender name before production can start, and that costs you days on your own lead time.

7. Currency

We invoice in US dollars as standard, and in euros or pounds sterling where that is agreed in the quotation. The currency on your invoice is the currency to pay in; paying a dollar invoice in another currency leaves a shortfall after conversion, which then has to be settled before dispatch.

8. A short security note, worth reading

Invoice fraud is common in this industry, and it works by intercepting email. Two rules protect you:

  • We only ever send payment links and invoices from info@fabrioza.com. Treat a payment request from any other address as fraudulent, however similar it looks.
  • Our bank details never change by email. If you receive a message claiming our account has changed, it did not come from us.

If anything about a payment request looks different from the last one — a new account, a new bank, a different country, an unusual urgency — stop and call or message WhatsApp +92 312 4815601 before sending anything. We would far rather answer that question than help you recover a misdirected wire, which is usually impossible.
